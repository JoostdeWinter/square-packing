Scalar comparison for packing unit squares in a square

If n squares fit in a square of side a+f, where a is an integer and 0<f<1, write e=n-a² for the excess over an a by a grid. The same excess notation works for a rectangle of sides i+f and j+f relative to i*j.

A rectangular gluing uses a packing in [0,i+f]×[0,j+f] and a second one of size (u+f)×(v+f), translated and rotated to use [i,i+u+f]×[j,j+v+f]. Their boxes share only the corner [i,i+f]×[j,j+f]. When either component avoids the interior of that corner, the joined packing is feasible. Fill [0,i]×[j+f,j+v+f] and [i+f,i+u+f]×[0,j] with integer grids. The resulting box has size (i+u+f)×(j+v+f), with excess the sum of the two input excesses. Its northwest and southeast corners each contain an upright unit square provided all four integer dimensions are positive.

Given an arbitrary first component, add a unit-wide strip on its right side holding j upright squares against the bottom. Its new northeast corner is empty, and excess is unchanged relative to its new integer dimensions i+1 and j. A horizontal strip gives the same guarantee, exchanging width and height. Thus two scalar square bounds a+f1 and b+f2 alone give a packing with excess e1+e2 and square side a+b+1+max(f1,f2). Choose f=max(f1,f2), perform the vertical-strip gluing above, then fill the final unit-high gap with upright squares. With t inputs and one strip at every joint, the strips can alternate between dimensions. This requires integer side sum(a_i)+ceil((t-1)/2). If the current component was made by gluing and has an upright in an exterior corner, removing that one square and reflecting the box creates a free corner for the next joint without using an extra strip. This costs one excess square. The construction may be repeated.

There is also a first-joint option with no spacer and no known shape. Put the two square packings in opposite corners of the proposed box, using their true bounds a+f1 and b+f2. At target fractional part f≥max(f1,f2) their overlap is a square of side d=max(0,f1+f2-f). Removing all tiles of one component that enter its overlapping corner makes the joint feasible. All centers of the tiles that meet that corner lie in a bounding square of side d+(sqrt(2)-1)/2, because the center stays at least 1/2 from each enclosing wall and at most sqrt(2)/2 past the corner neighborhood. Distinct tile centers are at least 1 apart since every tile contains its radius-1/2 inscribed disk.

No tile needs removal when d=0. When d<0.5 the center square's diameter is smaller than 1, so at most one tile requires removal. When d<2/sqrt(5)-(sqrt(2)-1)/2, bisecting the center square into two columns gives two subrectangles of diameter less than 1. When d<1-(sqrt(2)-1)/2, at most three centers are possible. Indeed four centers in convex position would have perimeter at least 4, exceeding the perimeter of the containing square. Perimeter of a convex polygon is monotone under inclusion. If one center is inside or on the triangle of the other three, the largest of the three angular gaps viewed from that center is at least 120 degrees and at most 180 degrees. The opposite side then has length at least sqrt(3), exceeding the box diagonal. Finally at any d<1 a 2 by 2 subdivision gives four subrectangles of diameter less than 1.

The comparison code spacers.py performs unbounded dynamic programming on integer side cost. Mark the initial tile and all tiles added with a spacer by q=1, and all others by q=0. An item costs 2*a+q and contributes e-1+q. To achieve integer side K the budget is 2*K+1, with at least two q=1 items in a construction of two or more components using a spacer. One q=1 item suffices for a singleton. The code also treats first gluing without a spacer using the conservative corner bound. fraction_pair.py explicitly checks the corner thresholds against individual fractional parts. The resulting bounds are then closed under deletion, square-array replication and L-border extension. Witness availability for the underlying Kaggle leaderboard scores is not assumed. These are consequences conditional on the packings reported by the cited sources.
